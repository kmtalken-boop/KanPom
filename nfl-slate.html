How to read this. Model lines are KanPom ratings (plus a backup-QB penalty when the starter is out). They are a power rating, not a market-beating forecast: over 2015-2025 a gap between the model and the closing line has not predicted results; usage shares and EPA/touch are rolling, shrunk values. Player usage, EPA and defense grades use games through Week 10.
Forward record (games settled after the fact): ATS 26-45-1, totals 37-35-0, 73 games; margin MAE model 10.36 vs market 9.281. Novig fair line vs ESPN as a predictor (59 games): margin error 8.66 vs 8.663, total error 11.078 vs 11.047.
Projected vs Midpoint. Projected is the props model's average outcome — read it for role and volume. A prop line is set near the number a player beats half the time, and an average sits above that because a few big games pull it up, so the page also shows the Midpoint: the projection cut by 17% for rushing yards, 20% for receiving yards and 11% for receptions (where real results landed against these projections in Weeks 5-9; passing yards needs no cut). Hold the Midpoint, never the Projected number, against a line. Over those weeks the Novig line was closer to the real result than the Midpoint on every stat, so the market line is the starting point: on yardage it IS the fair value, and on receptions the fair value moves 25% of the way from the line toward the Midpoint (hover a Midpoint cell to see it). An O or U tag marks a receptions Midpoint 15%+ from Novig's line: that gap went 78-46 in five weeks, a small sample that looked weaker (55-57%) under other ways of building the Midpoint — something to watch, not a bet. The Midpoint is below the line on about four of five yardage props; that reflects the size of the cut, and a yardage gap on its own has been a coin flip.
What-if toggles. Every player the injury report flags, and every skill player listed, has a PLAYS / SITS button. It recomputes the model line (about 0.6 points per non-QB starter, 3.4 if the regular QB sits), the win probability, the gap banner, and the projections and midpoints.
